# Shared vocabulary turns AI ambition into sound project choices

Question: What do managers and engineers need to understand before they scope an LLM or AI-agent project?

Situation: LLMs and agents are now practical building blocks, but teams often use their terms interchangeably.

Complication: Confusing capabilities, controls, and operating trade-offs leads to poorly scoped pilots, unmanaged risk, and brittle delivery.

Resolution: Treat an AI initiative as a system design problem: define the model boundaries, ground it in trusted knowledge, control its actions, measure quality and safety, and deliberately manage cost and latency.

Key line:
1. Model fundamentals define what the system can see and produce — evidence: primary technical documentation (have).
2. Grounding and action patterns make general models useful in an enterprise — evidence: RAG research and tool-use documentation (have).
3. Evaluation and guardrails turn capability into a controllable product — evidence: NIST and evaluation guidance (have).
4. Project teams must explicitly choose the quality, cost, and latency point they will optimize — evidence: model-system design guidance (have).

Open questions for the user: None. This is an introductory, vendor-neutral vocabulary deck; all examples are conceptual rather than client data.
