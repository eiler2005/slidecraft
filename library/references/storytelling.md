# Storytelling decks

Consulting decks persuade a decision maker with an answer first. Story decks change what an audience **remembers and does** — at a conference, an all-hands, a customer event, or when you share what you learned. The craft is different, and so is the deck.

## When to use which

| | Consulting pyramid | Story |
|---|---|---|
| Audience | A few decision makers | Many people, mixed context |
| Goal | A decision today | One idea remembered, one action tomorrow |
| Order | Answer → reasons → evidence | Hook → tension → resolution → insight |
| Slides | Dense, self-explanatory, sent as pre-read | Sparse, carried by the speaker |
| Titles | Action titles that argue | Short lines that land; the speaker carries the argument |

**Blend them** when presenting a recommendation live to a large group: open with a story beat (the customer moment), then give the answer and reasons, then return to the story for the call to action.

## The arc

1. **Hook** — a moment, a surprising number or a question that creates tension in the first minute. No agenda slide.
2. **Stakes** — who is affected and what is at risk. One concrete consequence beats five abstract ones.
3. **Journey** — what happened, in order, including what did not work. Obstacles make the insight credible.
4. **Turning point** — the moment things changed and why. This is the emotional peak; give it a slide of its own.
5. **Insight** — the transferable lesson as one quotable sentence, with up to three supporting lessons.
6. **Call to action** — one thing the audience can do tomorrow, and how to reach you.

## Slide patterns

| Beat | Layout · variant | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | `big-statement` · hero-number or statement | One number or sentence, huge. Silence after it. |
| Stakes | `big-statement` · quote, `kpi-tiles` · strip | A real voice (with permission) beats a statistic. |
| Journey | `timeline` · milestones, `text-image` | One beat per slide; keep the time axis clear. |
| Turning point | `big-statement`, `chart-takeaway` | Show the moment the line bends. |
| Insight | `big-statement` · statement, `key-points` · cards | The sentence you want quoted afterwards. |
| Proof | `chart-takeaway`, `kpi-tiles` | Show the lesson generalises; cite the source. |
| Call to action | `title` · closing | Imperative, specific, small. |

Themes that suit stories: `midnight` (keynote, dark room), `editorial` (warm, reflective), `modern-pitch` (energetic). Build the HTML output for presenting from a browser (`-f html`, presenter notes with `N`).

## Writing for the ear

- Short sentences; concrete nouns; active verbs. Read slides aloud before you keep them.
- Put the detail in speaker notes, not on the slide. The slide is a cue, not a script.
- Name people and places (with permission) — specificity is what makes a story believable.
- Numbers: one per slide, rounded, with a comparison ("two days, twelve times a year").

## Pitfalls

- **Starting with the agenda or the company history.** Start with the tension.
- **A happy path with no obstacles.** Without struggle the insight has no weight.
- **Three insights of equal size.** Pick one; the others support it.
- **Text-heavy slides read aloud.** If the slide needs to be read, it is a document — send it instead.
- **Vague calls to action** ("think about it"). Ask for one concrete next step.
